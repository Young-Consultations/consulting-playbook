# Consulting Editorial Strategy and Voice Guide

**Status:** Current working guidance as of 2026-08-14  
**Scope:** Young Consultations public-facing Substack and LinkedIn content

This guide preserves settled content decisions and evidence-based observations
from the author's consulting conversations. It guides public communication; it
does not define product requirements, architecture, consulting conclusions, or
publication authority. The [canonical content inventory](ideas/ideas-backlog.md)
remains the source of truth for post status and public URLs.

## Audience and point of view

Write primarily for engineering leaders and software teams trying to improve
software quality and throughput through disciplined engineering and practical AI
adoption.

The portfolio position is:

> Consulting discovers value. Slugger demonstrates and delivers it. Prompt
> engineering makes it repeatable. AI-SDLC is earned through validation.

Treat the consulting practice as the value-discovery and client-delivery engine,
Slugger as a demonstration and delivery accelerator, and the AI-SDLC as a body of
practice that must be supported by repeatable evidence. Do not imply that a full
AI-SDLC platform or a consulting result is proven merely because AI generated
code or a workflow passed a local check.

## Publication strategy

1. Seed several foundational Substack posts before beginning the steady weekly
   cadence.
2. Use LinkedIn for visibility, discussion, and concise derivatives of the
   long-form point of view.
3. Build connected posts rather than isolated commentary. Each post should
   establish one idea and create a natural bridge to the next.
4. Use a low-pressure early call to action: invite questions, disagreement,
   relevant team examples, or contact from readers facing the same problem.
5. Record publication status and URLs only in the canonical inventory. Record
   engagement measures with their observation window and source; never estimate
   missing historical values.

The first foundational sequence currently begins with `CONTENT-001` and
`CONTENT-009`. The closing bridge from `CONTENT-009` is tracked as `CONTENT-026`.

## Evidence and claims

- Lead with a real engineering problem, decision, or observed constraint.
- Distinguish a verified defect from an inference, lesson, or hypothesis.
- Generalize project experience without exposing sensitive or private evidence.
- Link repository evidence where practical, but do not treat a link as consent
  to reproduce its contents publicly.
- Prefer measures connected to defects, rework, escape distance, human
  intervention, or delivery outcomes over a generic “metrics-driven” claim.
- Do not present estimated rework avoided as measured savings.
- Keep a secondary point brief when developing it fully would distract from the
  main argument; place it in a later post instead.

## Authentic voice

The author's observed voice is direct, pragmatic, analytical, systems-minded,
iterative, collaborative, and quietly authoritative. It is approachable through
concrete questions and visible reasoning rather than emotional ornament.

### Writing rules

- Lead with the problem, point, recommendation, or decision.
- Show constraints, tradeoffs, downstream effects, and practical consequences.
- Alternate short execution statements with longer exploratory sentences. A
  blended average of roughly 16–22 words is a guide, not a target to optimize.
- Natural connectors include “And,” “But,” “So,” “Which is why,” and “Based on
  this.” Use them only where the transition is real.
- Use technical terms when they identify an actual mechanism or artifact.
- Prefer plain functional language: “use” over “leverage,” “change” over
  “transform,” and “works together” over “creates synergy.”
- Use formatting when it clarifies a mapping, sequence, owner, risk, or decision.
- End with an implication, decision, focused question, next action, or specific
  invitation to engage.

When making a recommendation, state it, explain the governing constraint, name
the risk or rework it prevents, connect it to the customer, team, MVP, or system,
and identify the next action. Do not bury the recommendation in background or
claim certainty without evidence.

## Editing boundary

Polish grammar and clarity without sanding away the reasoning. Correct dictation
errors, run-on sentences, misspellings, repetition, and redundant clauses rather
than imitating them. As a practical guide, edit about 15–20% beyond the author's
natural draft.

Avoid corporate fluff, AI or business hype, canned LinkedIn hooks, robotic
transitions, emojis, excessive em dashes, empty praise, generic endings, and
formulaic “It's not X; it's Y” contrasts. Avoid phrases such as “synergize,”
“revolutionary,” unsupported “10x,” “Hot take,” “Let that sink in,” “In
conclusion,” and “Hope this helps.”

## Pre-publication check

- Does the opening state the real problem or point?
- Is the main claim supported and appropriately qualified?
- Are examples traceable, generalized, and safe to publish?
- Is any secondary idea better saved for a later post?
- Does the piece sound direct and human rather than corporate or AI-generated?
- Does the ending identify what follows or invite a specific kind of response?
- Has the author reviewed and made the final publication decision?
